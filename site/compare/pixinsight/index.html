Astraios vs PixInsight

A fair comparison between a free alpha program and the most established commercial astrophotography suite. Checked against official sources on 1 September 2026: Astraios 0.1.25 (v0.1.25-alpha) and PixInsight 1.9.4 "Lockhart". PixInsight's site refused automated access on the day of checking, so its price, trial and system requirements were taken from search-engine copies of the official pages and the official developer forum; verify on pixinsight.com before relying on exact wording.

Short version. Choose PixInsight for maturity, depth and an ecosystem of plugins and tutorials. Choose Astraios if you want a free, local-first, GPU-accelerated tool with a guided workflow and built-in deconvolution and AI denoise, and you accept alpha-quality software. Similar feature names do not mean equal results.

About GPU claims

"GPU acceleration" is only meaningful with a definition. Here it means the program's own processing engine running on the GPU. By that definition PixInsight's core does not document GPU use as of 1.9.4, while RC Astro's plugins (StarXTerminator, NoiseXTerminator, BlurXTerminator) do use the GPU inside the plugin; they are separate products (59.95 to 99.95 USD each on rc-astro.com on the day of checking). Astraios runs its own engine on CUDA or Apple Metal for the stages listed on the benchmarks page and on the CPU for the rest, with the same output either way.

What Astraios does not have

  • Years of refinement: PixInsight's algorithms and interface have been tuned since 2004; Astraios is months old.
  • An ecosystem: no third-party plugins, few tutorials, no forum beyond GitHub Discussions.
  • Camera raw support and code-signed installers.
